| Phase | Modules | Theme | Why here |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Foundations and linear patterns | 1–3 | Complexity, arrays, hashing, two pointers, sliding window, prefix sums, binary search, sorting | Roughly 40% of interview problems reduce to these. Everything later assumes you can analyse complexity and use a hash map reflexively |
| 2. Pointers and recursion | 4–6 | Linked lists, stacks, queues, monotonic stack, recursion, backtracking, trees | Pointer rewiring trains careful state handling; recursion is the engine of trees, backtracking, DFS and DP |
| 3. Structures and graphs | 7–9 | Heaps, intervals, tries, graph traversal, topological sort, Dijkstra, Union-Find, greedy | Heaps need tree intuition; Dijkstra needs heaps and BFS; greedy proofs use sorting and intervals |
| 4. DP and interview readiness | 10–12 | Dynamic programming, bit manipulation, mixed timed practice, mocks | DP needs recursion, memoisation and maturity. The final module converts knowledge into timed, unlabeled performance |
