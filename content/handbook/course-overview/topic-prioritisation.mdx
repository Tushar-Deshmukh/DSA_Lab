| Topic | Category | Placement | Reason |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Intervals | 1. Essential | Module 7 lesson 4 | Merging, scheduling and overlap questions are common at product companies and reuse sorting |
| Monotonic stack | 1. Essential | Module 4 lesson 5 | "Next greater/smaller" appears often and has a non-obvious O(n) solution |
| Union-Find applications | 1. Essential | Module 9 lesson 2 | Connectivity and redundant-edge problems; short to implement |
| Design problems (LRU and similar) | 1. Essential | Module 4 lesson 3, Module 12 lesson 2 | Product companies favour data-structure design that combines a map with a list |
| Tries | 1. Essential (one lesson) | Module 7 lesson 5 | Autocomplete and word search; the structure is simple and frequently tested |
| Monotonic deque | 2. If time | Worked example in Module 4 (the Module 4 theory, Example 4); practice via 239 as a Module 12 Hard option | Less frequent; builds directly on the monotonic stack |
| Advanced DP (tree DP, bitmask DP, interval DP) | 2. If time | After the course | High effort for lower frequency; tree DP is the first to add |
| Shortest paths beyond Dijkstra (Bellman-Ford via 787) | 2. If time | Module 9 optional | Appears occasionally; Dijkstra covers most cases |
| Math (GCD, fast power, sieve, modular arithmetic) | 2. If time | Fast power in Module 5 (50); rest in section 12 | Rarely the core of a question but useful helpers |
| Advanced graphs (Floyd-Warshall, Tarjan SCC, bridges, max-flow) | 3. Defer | Section 12 | Rare outside specialised roles |
| Segment and Fenwick trees | 3. Defer | Section 12 | Uncommon in product-company rounds; high implementation cost |
| String algorithms (KMP, Rabin-Karp, Z) | 3. Defer | Section 12 | Asked rarely; brute force plus hashing usually suffices |
| Reservoir sampling and randomised algorithms | 3. Defer | Section 12 | Niche; learn the idea in 30 minutes later |
