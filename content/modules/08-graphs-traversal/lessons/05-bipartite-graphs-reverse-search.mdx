why: Two-colouring is a clean example of forced choices propagating through a graph. Reverse search is the trick that turns 417 and 130 from O((m·n)²) into O(m·n), and it is easy to miss under pressure.
prereqs:
  - BFS and components (lessons 1–3)

  - Two-colour a graph with BFS, starting from every uncoloured node
  - Explain why a colouring conflict means an odd cycle
  - Search from the targets with the reversed condition (uphill from oceans, inward from borders)
ready:
  - 785 handles disconnected graphs
  - 417 runs two searches from the oceans
  - I two-coloured a 4-cycle and a triangle by hand
summary:
  - Bipartite ⇔ no odd cycle ⇔ a 2-colouring exists.
  - BFS from each uncoloured node; neighbour gets `1 − colour[u]`; same colour = conflict.
  - Many sources asking "can I reach the target?" → search from the target with the reversed rule.
  - '417: two uphill searches from each ocean; intersect. 130: mark border-connected O''s; flip the rest.'
  - O(V + E), O(m·n) on grids.
