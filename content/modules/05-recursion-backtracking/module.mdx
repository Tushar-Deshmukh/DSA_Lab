<Unit id="how-this-module-fits-together" kind="overview" title="How this module fits together">

Recursion is the first technique where you are not supposed to trace everything in your head. You write down what a call *returns*, trust the recursive calls to keep that promise, and check only the base case and one step. Backtracking applies the same trust to search: make a choice, explore, undo it.

| Lesson | Topic | The question you answer before coding |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Recursion, call stack, recursion trees | What does `f(state)` return? |
| 2 | Subsets and duplicates | What decision does each level make? |
| 3 | Permutations, combinations, pruning | What do I need to know to make the rest? |
| 4 | Combination Sum, letter combinations | Can an element be reused? |
| 5 | Grids and strings | What do I mark, and when do I restore it? |

The checkpoint is **Mock 1**, the first timed interview with a rubric.
