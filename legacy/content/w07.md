## Heapify in O(n) {theory}

To build a heap from n unordered elements, n pushes cost O(n log n). Sifting down every non-leaf, from the last one to the root, costs O(n): most nodes are near the bottom and can only move a level or two.

@viz heapify

```ex

tag: Why O(n)?
q: "Which argument shows that bottom-up heapify is O(n)?"
options:
  - t: "About n/2 nodes are leaves (0 work), n/4 can sift down at most 1 level, n/8 at most 2, … and n/4·1 + n/8·2 + n/16·3 + … ≤ n."
    ok: true
    why: "The deep sift-downs only happen for the few nodes near the root."
  - t: "Each sift-down is O(1)."
    why: "The root's sift-down can be O(log n). The point is that few nodes are that high."
    kind: concept
  - t: "It is actually O(n log n), the same as n pushes."
    why: "Pushing n times is O(n log n); sifting down from the bottom is O(n)."
    kind: concept
explain: "Most of a complete tree's nodes are near the leaves, and they do the least work."
```

## Keeping the most intervals: sort by end {theory}

Non-overlapping Intervals (435) asks for the minimum number of removals, which is the same as keeping as many compatible intervals as possible. Sort by **end** and keep every interval that starts at or after the end of the last one kept. Why end and not start: the interval that ends first leaves the most room for everything after it, so replacing any optimal solution's first interval with it never makes things worse (the activity-selection exchange argument, proven in Week 9).
